The problem
The world our customers live in

Rate cases used to reward a well-constructed plan. They now reward a well-defended one.

Utilities must justify every investment to a regulator in a legal proceeding called a rate case. Opposing parties challenge it. A commission decides what gets approved. One poorly defended project can unravel an entire program.

Even after filing, challengers send discovery requests with ten-day windows. A new law can reprice the plan overnight. A new commissioner can raise the evidence bar. The plan keeps evolving while the filed record stays frozen. The gap between what was filed and what the utility currently believes is where disallowance risk lives. Utilities holding up under this pressure have moved from explaining their decisions to proving them.

30-second pitch

Rate cases used to reward a well-constructed plan. They now reward a well-defended one. The difference comes down to a single shift: from explaining capital decisions to proving them. That requires cited evidence, a record of assumptions that doesn't change between filings, and a way to show that the same logic holds across every proceeding. Regulatory Intelligence is the structured assumption register that makes this possible. It's built on the investment data Copperleaf already holds — which is what makes every claim traceable back to a real decision.

One-line description

A regulatory workflow that connects live regulatory events to the investments Copperleaf manages for utilities — giving teams an evidence base that is structured, sourced, and consistent enough to hold up under cross-examination.

The defining distinction

Explain vs. prove. Explaining is narrative. Proving is cited evidence with a versioned trail. This is the shift the market has made — and the one Regulatory Intelligence enables.

What it is NOT

Not a replacement for regulatory strategy or legal counsel. Not a system of record for the entire capital plan. Not a guarantee of rate recovery. It supports the team's strategy for the investments that run through Copperleaf — it does not own that strategy.

Core unfair advantage

No other vendor holds the structured investment data — the value drivers, assumptions, and benefit-cost logic — for these customers. That data is what makes a structured assumption register meaningful. Without it, you can monitor regulatory signals, but you can't tell which filed assumptions they actually affect.
