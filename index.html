The world our customers live in

Rate cases used to reward a well-constructed plan. They now reward a well-defended one.

Utilities are not like normal businesses. Every significant investment — a new power line, a gas pipe replacement, a grid upgrade — must be formally justified to a state or federal regulator, who decides whether customers are allowed to pay for it. That process is called a rate case. It is part business plan, part legal proceeding.

Opposing parties challenge the utility's investments. A commission of appointed regulators decides what gets approved and what gets cut. One poorly defended project can trigger a line of questioning that unravels an entire program.

The pressure never fully lifts. Even after filing, challengers send formal discovery requests with ten-day windows. A new law can reprice the whole plan overnight. A newly appointed commissioner can raise the evidence bar without warning.

And here is the deeper problem: the capital plan keeps evolving while the filed record stays frozen. The gap between what a utility filed and what it currently believes is where disallowance risk lives. Intervenors have noticed. So have commissioners. The consistency gap between filings — between the IRP, the rate case, and the discovery response — is now one of the most common paths to disallowance.

The utilities holding up under this pressure have made a quiet shift. They have moved from explaining their capital decisions to proving them.

WATCH — Monitoring & Signal Translation
The pain

When a legislative amendment or ruling changes rate recovery rules, it doesn't just affect future projects — it creates a consistency gap between what was already filed and what the plan now says. Teams have no structured way to identify which filed assumptions are now inconsistent, which projects are exposed, or what a challenger will find when they cross-reference filings.

Who feels this

Capital Planning Manager, Regulatory Strategy Lead, VP Regulatory

How Regulatory Intelligence addresses this

The Regulatory Signal Monitor ingests legislative bills, commission rulings, and docket activity in near real-time via HData across 50+ state PUCs and FERC. The Plan Impact Translation Engine maps each signal to the specific assumptions it affects, identifies which filed positions are now inconsistent, and surfaces what needs to change before the next filing window closes.

Value delivered

The consistency gap is closed before a challenger finds it. Teams arrive at filing windows with a versioned, reconciled assumption register — not a reconstruction exercise under pressure.

Real example
Indiana HB1002 (2023) amended TDSIC cost recovery rules overnight. Duke Energy Indiana, NIPSCO, and AES Indiana had to manually re-examine which projects still cleared the benefit-cost threshold — with no system connecting the bill text to the filed assumptions it invalidated.
The pain

In most US states, commission members are appointed by the governor — meaning election outcomes directly influence the evidence standard within a utility's 7-year capital program. There is no systematic way to score this risk against a pending filing before it is submitted, or to identify which filed assumptions are most exposed to a new commissioner's posture.

Who feels this

VP Regulatory, Regulatory Strategy Lead

How Regulatory Intelligence addresses this

The Jurisdictional Configuration Engine tracks each commissioner's prior rulings, affordability posture, appointing governor, and term dates via HData. When composition changes, the system scores pending filings for exposure — not just which projects are at risk, but which assumptions in the structured register are most likely to be challenged based on the new commissioner's history.

Value delivered

The team knows what the new commissioner will look for before the filing is submitted. The assumption register already holds the versioned, cited evidence that addresses that posture — strategy instead of triage.

Real example
A new IURC commissioner with a sharper affordability focus contributed to NIPSCO's recovery request being cut by $41M. That outcome was predictable from prior rulings — but nobody had indexed those positions against the filed assumptions before submission.
The pain

Multi-jurisdiction filers face compounding consistency risk. What the IRP said, what the rate case said, and what the discovery response says are supposed to tell one consistent story. They rarely do — not because the work is sloppy, but because the plan keeps moving while each filing stays frozen at the moment it was submitted. Intervenors cross-reference filings. The gaps are found.

How Regulatory Intelligence addresses this

Active proceedings are monitored simultaneously across all relevant PUCs and FERC. As each ruling lands, the Plan Impact Translation Engine assesses its effect on the structured assumption register, flags which filed positions are now inconsistent, and maintains one reconciled version of the evidence base across all active proceedings.

Value delivered

One consistent evidence base across every active proceeding. Challengers find a coherent narrative, not a consistency gap.

Real example
Ameren Illinois faced four simultaneous ICC adverse orders between December 2023 and January 2026 while $31.8B in committed capital sat in an unresolved recovery regime. No tool was tracking all four threads and reconciling the filed assumptions as each ruling landed.
DEFEND — Filing Preparation & Discovery Response
Govern first: Defend capabilities are the downstream consequence of having a structured assumption register in place. Lead with the Govern thesis — Defend becomes compelling once the buyer understands that the register is what makes fast, cited, defensible responses possible.
The pain

Intervenor requests arrive with ten-day windows. Answering them requires pulling data from multiple disconnected systems across dozens of engineers under pressure. The underlying knowledge exists inside the utility — it just isn't structured, versioned, or cited in a way that holds up under cross-examination. One slow, poorly grounded response contributed to a 30% reduction in approved capital investment at a major US utility.

Who feels this

Regulatory Strategy Lead, Regulatory Affairs Attorney

How Regulatory Intelligence addresses this

The Intervenor Request Accelerator draws on the structured assumption register to generate cited draft responses in hours. Every response traces to a specific source field and data record in the register. If a question cannot be answered from the existing record, the system flags it rather than generating an unsupported answer. Attorneys review and approve — nothing enters the proceeding without explicit sign-off.

Value delivered

Discovery becomes a workflow, not a war room. Response time drops from 3 to 5 days to under 4 hours. The attorney review queue replaces a chaotic cross-team scramble — and every answer traces to the same versioned register the IRP and rate case were built from.

Real example
Indiana's OUCC systematically challenged Duke Energy's value model methodology. The Commission directed Duke toward greater transparency — now a hard constraint on every subsequent filing. The consistency between methodology in the register and what appears in testimony is what the next proceeding will test.

Engineers spend a month or more pulling together information already in Copperleaf — with no structured template, no reusable output, and no citeable trail back to the assumptions that drove the decision. The same effort repeats every cycle. The knowledge is there. It just isn't structured.

Value delivered

Months of manual assembly reduced to days of review. One structured, versioned evidence base — built once, cited always.
