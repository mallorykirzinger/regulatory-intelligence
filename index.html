The world our customers live in

Every dollar a utility spends has to be approved by a regulator — and defended in a legal proceeding.

Utilities are not like normal businesses. They cannot simply invest in their infrastructure and pass the cost on to customers. Every significant investment — a new power line, a gas pipe replacement, a grid upgrade — must be formally justified to a state or federal regulator, who decides whether customers are allowed to pay for it.

That process is called a rate case. It is part business plan, part legal proceeding. The utility presents its investment program. Opposing parties — consumer advocates, large industrial customers, sometimes competing utilities — challenge it. A commission of appointed regulators decides what gets approved and what gets cut.

The stakes are enormous. A single rate case can cover billions of dollars of investment. One poorly defended project can trigger a line of questioning that unravels an entire program. Teams of engineers, planners, and lawyers work for months — sometimes through the night — to build the case.

And the pressure never fully lifts. Even after filing, challengers can send formal discovery requests demanding detailed answers within ten days. A new law can reprice the whole plan overnight. A newly appointed commissioner can change the evidence standard entirely. The regulatory environment shifts constantly while the filed record stays frozen.

At the same time, these teams are expected to balance three competing demands: keep customer bills affordable, maintain aging infrastructure, and invest in a modernizing grid. There is no slack. Every decision carries a burden of proof. And getting it wrong costs millions.

WATCH — Monitoring & Signal Translation
The pain

Regulatory events such as legislative amendments, new court rulings, or changes to rate recovery rules can instantly reprice a utility's entire investment program. Capital planning teams have no structured system to identify which projects are now at risk, by how much, or what to do next. The analysis happens manually and too late, by which time the exposure has often already become a real financial problem.

Who feels this

Capital Planning Manager, Regulatory Strategy Lead, VP Regulatory

How Regulatory Intelligence addresses this

The Regulatory Signal Monitor ingests legislative bills, commission rulings, and docket activity in near real-time from HData's feed covering 50+ state public utility commissions and FERC. The Plan Impact Translation Engine then maps each signal to the specific investments it affects, shows the direction of exposure, and surfaces recommended next steps before the next filing window closes.

Value delivered

Exposure is identified in hours, not weeks. Teams arrive at filing windows prepared with a ranked action list rather than discovering problems during the proceeding itself.

Real example
Indiana HB1002 (2023) amended TDSIC cost recovery rules overnight. Duke Energy Indiana, NIPSCO, and AES Indiana all had to manually re-examine which projects still cleared the required benefit-cost threshold under the new rules. No system connected the bill text to the affected investments automatically.
The pain

In most US states, utility commission members are appointed by the governor, meaning elections directly influence the regulatory environment within a utility's 7-year capital program. A commissioner with a sharper focus on customer affordability changes the evidence standard for the entire proceeding. There is no systematic way today to score this risk against a pending filing before it is submitted.

Who feels this

VP Regulatory, Regulatory Strategy Lead

How Regulatory Intelligence addresses this

The Jurisdictional Configuration Engine, fed by HData's commissioner profiles, tracks each commissioner's prior rulings, affordability posture, appointing governor, and term dates. When commission composition changes, the system scores the customer's pending filings for exposure and surfaces filing strategy implications before submission.

Value delivered

A commissioner change no longer catches the team off guard. Filing strategy adapts before submission rather than after an unexpected cut to the approved capital ask.

Real example
A new Indiana Utility Regulatory Commission commissioner with a sharper affordability focus contributed to NIPSCO's investment recovery request being cut by $41M. That outcome was predictable from the commissioner's prior rulings, but nobody had indexed those positions against the pending filing before it was submitted.
The pain

Large utilities regularly manage multiple simultaneous regulatory proceedings across different jurisdictions. Each ruling changes recovery assumptions, but there is no tool that tracks all threads together and recalculates total capital exposure as each ruling lands. Teams piece this together manually from separate docket files.

Who feels this

How Regulatory Intelligence addresses this

Active proceedings are monitored across all relevant state PUCs and FERC simultaneously via HData's docket feed. Value delivered

The total exposure envelope is always current and visible in one place, rather than being reconstructed retroactively from separate docket files under pressure.

Real example
Ameren Illinois faced four simultaneous Illinois Commerce Commission adverse orders between December 2023 and January 2026, while $31.8B in committed capital sat in an unresolved recovery regime. A concurrent FERC ruling threatened a further $19M per year in impact. No single tool tracked all four threads and recalculated exposure as each ruling landed.
DEFEND — Filing Preparation & Discovery Response
The pain

Intervenor discovery requests (formal questions from opposing parties such as consumer advocates or other utilities) arrive with response windows as short as ten days. Answering them requires pulling data from multiple disconnected systems and coordinating across dozens of engineers under pressure. Errors or inconsistencies in responses open new lines of legal challenge. One slow, poorly grounded response contributed to a 30% reduction in approved capital investment at a major US utility.

Who feels this

Regulatory Strategy Lead, Regulatory Affairs Attorney

How Regulatory Intelligence addresses this

The Intervenor Request Accelerator generates draft responses in hours, fully cited against the filed record. If a question cannot be answered from the existing record, the system flags it rather than generating an unsupported answer. Attorneys review each draft before anything is submitted. The system enforces sign-off as a hard workflow gate, not a suggestion. Prior responses are also searchable to surface recurring challenge patterns.

Value delivered

Response time drops from 3 to 5 days to under 4 hours. No unsupported claims reach the record. The attorney review queue replaces a chaotic cross-team scramble under litigation pressure.

Real example
Indiana's Office of Utility Consumer Counselor systematically challenged Duke Energy's investment value model methodology across proceedings. The Commission directed Duke toward greater transparency in its next filing cycle, now a hard constraint on how the plan must be prepared. Projects with weaker benefit-cost ratios carry materially higher challenge risk because of this precedent.
The pain

Each filing cycle requires individual written justifications for 75 or more capital projects. Engineers spend a month or more pulling together information that already exists in Copperleaf, formatting it for regulatory purposes, and writing justifications with no structured template or reusable output. The work repeats every cycle from scratch.

Who feels this

Engineers and Project Owners, Capital Planning Manager

How Regulatory Intelligence addresses this

The Project Justification Evidence Base auto-generates a first-draft written justification for every project in the filing, sourced from existing Copperleaf data. Each justification links project scope, value drivers, timing rationale, and statutory alignment. Engineers review and enrich rather than building from scratch. This evidence base also becomes the permanent, citable foundation for fast discovery responses throughout the proceeding.

Value delivered

Months of manual assembly are replaced by minutes of generation and days of review. The evidence base is reusable, consistently formatted, and citable across every proceeding.

The pain

Capital plans continue to evolve after a filing is submitted. When discovery requests arrive months later asking why specific values changed or what the plan showed on filing day, teams have to reconstruct their analysis under litigation pressure. This reconstruction introduces inconsistencies and creates new legal exposure.

Who feels this

Capital Planning Manager, Regulatory Affairs Attorney, Regulatory Strategy Lead

How Regulatory Intelligence addresses this

The Filing Snapshot Service captures a permanent, immutable copy of the capital plan at the exact moment of filing, along with a queryable log of every change made since. Answering "what were your numbers on filing day" becomes a minutes-long query rather than a reconstruction exercise.

Value delivered

Eliminates reconstructed analysis under legal pressure. Every discovery response is anchored to an auditable, immutable record that attorneys can trust and cite with confidence.
